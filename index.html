Cook these in the Instant Pot app

Every recipe here can be pulled into {{ site.appliance.app }} — the app that pairs with the {{ site.appliance.short }} — using its Recipe Import feature, which takes recipes from any website. Once imported, the app walks you through the recipe step by step.

  1. Open the recipe you want and copy its link — each page has a Copy recipe link button near the top.
  2. In {{ site.appliance.app }}, find Recipe Import and paste the link.
  3. Check the imported ingredients and timings against the page before you start cooking.

The same link works in Paprika, Mela, Crouton and AnyList, because each page carries its recipe a second time in schema.org/Recipe form — machine-readable data embedded in the page, the same format Google reads to build a recipe card in search results.

Worth knowing: importers reconstruct a recipe from the page and can get a detail wrong, so glance over the ingredients and times after importing. The step wording here is written to survive that trip — each step restates its Sauté or Pressure Cook setting rather than assuming the previous step is still on screen.

How these recipes are written

Calibrated for one cooker
Timings assume an {{ site.appliance.name }} ({{ site.appliance.watts }}, also on Amazon), which preheats faster and runs hotter on Sauté than a Duo. They still work on other pressure cookers — where the difference matters, the recipe says so and gives you the adjustment.
Every step names its setting
Each instruction is tagged with the function, level and duration — Sauté · Medium · 8 min — so you are never guessing which button to press or how long to hold it there.
Steps that stand alone
A step continuing an existing Sauté session repeats that context instead of assuming you remember it. Recipe apps read each step in isolation, so this is what keeps the appliance settings intact after an import.
Allergens against a published list
Every recipe is checked against the fourteen allergens of Annex II to Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, the list a European food label has to emphasise. That list contains all nine of the US FDA’s major allergens, so one declaration answers both. Each page says what the dish contains, what it may contain and which of the fourteen it is free of — and the free-of list is worked out by subtracting the first two from the published fourteen, so it can never quietly skip one.
Weights, not cups
Ingredients are given in grams and millilitres. In a sealed pot the ratio of rice to water decides the result, and a cup of rice is not a reliable amount.
The failure modes, up front
Where a step commonly goes wrong — a Burn warning from an unscraped base, spices catching on High — the recipe says what happens and why, at the point where it matters.
